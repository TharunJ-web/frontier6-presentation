Challenge: Space Mission Design Game

01 · The problem

People see the launch, not the trade-offs behind it

Every mission balances six budgets. Change one, and everything else moves.

That’s hard to learn from a textbook, and almost nobody gets to try it.

02 · Our approach

Every choice has a cost

Insight

Mission planning is all trade-offs: better instruments need more mass, power and money.

Decision

Build the game around live budgets that react to every part the player picks.

Result

Players learn why missions are designed the way they are by succeeding and failing.

How the game scores a mission

40%Science
25%Objectives
15%Efficiency
10%Reliability
10%Innovation

Mission report: “Your instruments generate far more data than your comms can downlink.” Scores of 90+ earn SUCCESS.

03 · The solution

Space Mission Design Game

Design, launch and fly a full space mission, then see why it worked or failed.

01

Design: pick a scenario, then build the spacecraft: power, comms, attitude control, avionics and instruments.

02

Plan: choose an orbit, a launch vehicle and one of 8 destinations, with live orbit data from NASA’s GMAT.

03

Fly: launch, cruise and operate month by month, through solar flares, outages and other random events.

04

Learn: a 0–100 score and a report that explains each trade-off in plain language.

05 · NASA data we used

Real numbers drive every mission

Orbit physics

7.67 km/s

NASA GMAT computes live velocity, period, semi-major axis and eccentricity. Low Earth orbit at 400 km: one lap every 92.6 min.

Spacecraft parts

<10% margin

Small Spacecraft Technology data sets each part’s mass, power and cost. Mass or budget under 10% spare, or power under 15%, gets flagged.

Orbits

6 orbits

LEO, sun-synchronous, GEO at 35,786 km, Sun–Earth L1, low lunar and Mars orbit, each with real coverage, drag and radiation.

Launch vehicles

3 tiers

Light, medium and heavy rockets from NASA Launch Services data. Payload class decides which of 8 destinations you can reach.
